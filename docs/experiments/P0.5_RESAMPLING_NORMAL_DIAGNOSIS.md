# P0.5 resampling / normal diagnosis and E1 fusion ablation (2026-11-03)

Status: Part A (image space) complete; Part B (mesh space) in progress.
This document is updated incrementally after each experiment.

## Question

P0 measured a 1.24% mean relative depth error but a 32.16° mean surface-normal
error for VGGT-Omega on the frozen 16-view Lego protocol, and both
`pose_depth_ablation.py` (depth regrid, `cv2.INTER_NEAREST`) and `tsdf_mesh.py`
(voxel projection, nearest pixel) sample with nearest neighbour. Part A asks how
much of the 32.16° is introduced by resampling/grid alignment versus being
intrinsic to the network output. Part B asks the same for the TSDF fusion stage
and tests robust fusion variants (E1).

Protocol is unchanged from P0: same 16 training views, GT intrinsics, one
train-only Sim(3), the frozen GT diagnostic grid (voxel 0.01150144, truncation 4
voxels, uniform weights). GT depth is used for evaluation only, never as an
optimization input. All new behaviour lives behind parameterized switches whose
defaults reproduce the P0 pipeline exactly.

## Part A — image-space resampling/normal diagnosis

Command:

```powershell
.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/diagnostics/resampling_normal_ablation.py --part a
```

Conditions per frame, aggregated over all 16 views (1.27M valid depth pixels,
1.18M valid normal pixels; flat = GT normal variation <2° over the 5-px stencil,
50,759 px; edge = GT depth discontinuity/silhouette band, 162,165 px):

| Condition | depth abs-rel mean / median | normal mean° | flat-region mean° | edge-region mean° |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A1 nearest regrid (P0 protocol) | 1.2416% / 0.701% | **32.156** | 15.608 | 33.860 |
| A2 bilinear regrid | 1.2438% / 0.702% | 32.073 | 15.491 | 33.630 |
| A3 normals on native Omega grid (no resampling at all) | — (shares A2 correspondence) | 32.050 | 15.432 | 33.936 |
| A5 nearest regrid, radius-1 stencil | — | 32.678 | 16.517 | 35.269 |
| A4 bilateral σc=0.02 → nearest regrid | 1.2396% / 0.700% | 32.000 | 15.214 | 34.095 |
| A4 bilateral σc=0.05 → nearest regrid | 1.2392% / 0.701% | 32.037 | 15.267 | 33.973 |
| A4 bilateral σc=0.10 → nearest regrid | 1.2410% / 0.704% | 32.093 | 15.316 | 33.752 |
| A6 depth error measured on the native grid | 2.355% / **0.697%** (median ≈ A1) | — | — | — |

A6 note: GT depth is bilinearly sampled onto the Omega grid (all four taps
required inside the GT foreground), so the predicted map itself is never
resampled. Its median (0.697%) matches A1 (0.701%); the inflated mean comes from
a heavy tail of near-silhouette mismatches caused by the known extrinsic offset,
not by resampling.

Error maps: `deliverables/lego_p05/resampling_maps.png`
(rows = frames r_0/r_33/r_59/r_79; columns = A1 depth error, A1/A2/A3/A4 normal
error). Visual review (by the agent model listed below): the normal-error
pattern is dense pixel-scale mottling covering the whole object, and it is
**visually identical** across the A1/A2/A3/A4 columns — no stripe, staircase or
moiré structure appears in any column, and switching interpolation does not
change the pattern. A nearest-regrid staircase would have shown a periodic
block pattern tied to the ~1.0008×/0.9957× intrinsic warp and would visibly
change between nearest and bilinear; neither is observed.

### Part A findings

1. **Resampling contributes ~0.1° of the 32.16° (≈0.3%).** Nearest → bilinear:
   32.156° → 32.073°. Removing resampling entirely (normals computed on the
   native Omega grid): 32.050°. The regrid hypothesis is rejected.
2. **The stencil operator is not the problem.** Radius 1 instead of 2 makes it
   slightly worse (32.68°), i.e. the 5-px stencil is not amplifying noise.
3. **Edge-preserving filtering cannot fix it.** Bilateral sweeps
   σc ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.10} (σs=3, d=5) move the mean by ≤0.16° and the flat
   region by ≤0.39°, with no visible change in the error maps.
4. **The predicted depth is locally smooth — smoother than GT.** Pixel-scale
   roughness |d − median5(d)|/d on interior foreground: Omega median 0.0096%,
   p90 0.173%; GT depth itself: median 0.067%, p90 0.314% (GT is "rougher" at
   pixel scale because ray-traced CAD depth follows exact slanted planes).
5. **The error is not pixel-scale iid noise.** Simulating GT depth + iid
   lognormal noise with the observed median magnitude (σ=0.7%) yields a
   flat-region normal error of 52.9°, far above the measured 15.6°.

Conclusion of Part A: the normal error is dominated by **mid-frequency surface
shape error** — smooth warps of the predicted surface at ~20–100 px wavelength
with sub-percent depth amplitude. Such warps leave depth statistics (~1.24%)
and pixel roughness (~0.01%) almost untouched while tilting local normals by
tens of degrees. They cannot be removed by interpolation choice, stencil
choice, or small-window edge-preserving filters; candidate remedies are
multi-view consistency (robust fusion — tested in Part B) or RGB-guided depth
refinement (P1).

## Part B — mesh-space ablation on the frozen P0 grid

Command:

```powershell
.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/diagnostics/resampling_normal_ablation.py --part b
blender -b --disable-autoexec --python scripts/diagnostics/measure_surfaces.py -- --out deliverables/lego_p05
```

All cases use the identical GT diagnostic grid (voxel 0.01150144, truncation 4
voxels, origin/shape asserted equal to the P0 report), uniform weights, no
consistency filter. `repro_nearest` reproduces the P0 `pred_pose_pred_depth`
case exactly (234,215 vertices / 406,187 faces / 61,463 boundary edges;
F-score 0.6206, accuracy 1.403, completeness 0.809 — bit-identical), which
validates the refactored fusion path.

| Case | F-score@1vox ↑ | precision / recall@1vox | accuracy (vox) ↓ | completeness (vox) ↓ | mesh normal mean° ↓ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| repro_nearest (= P0 baseline) | **0.6206** | 0.554 / 0.705 | 1.403 | 0.809 | 51.28 |
| bilinear_regrid | 0.6184 | 0.553 / 0.701 | 1.408 | 0.812 | 51.08 |
| bilinear_fusion | 0.6177 | 0.553 / 0.699 | 1.408 | 0.817 | 50.65 |
| bilinear_both | 0.6146 | 0.550 / 0.697 | 1.425 | 0.822 | 50.79 |
| bilateral (σc=0.02) | 0.6177 | 0.553 / 0.699 | 1.411 | 0.820 | 51.30 |
| robust_fusion (Huber δ=0.3) | 0.6185 | 0.555 / 0.698 | 1.410 | 0.816 | 50.86 |
| combined (all of the above) | 0.6102 | 0.550 / 0.686 | 1.441 | 0.836 | 50.49 |
| gt_pose + bilinear_fusion | 0.5968 | 0.542 / 0.664 | 1.395 | 0.863 | 50.51 |

### Part B findings

1. **No fusion-side variant improves on the baseline.** Every ablation lands
   within −0.01 F-score of 0.6206; the full combination is the worst
   (0.6102). Interpolation, filtering and robust fusion are all neutral to
   slightly negative on this data.
2. **Robust (Huber) fusion does not remove the waviness either** (0.6185 vs
   0.6206, accuracy unchanged). Since a Huber pass suppresses views that
   disagree with the consensus, its failure tells us the depth deviations are
   **consistent across views** — 16-view averaging has nothing to average
   away. This rules out per-view independent noise as the cause.
3. Bilinear fusion with GT pose is slightly *worse* than the nearest P0 case
   (0.5968 vs 0.6021): interpolation marginally blurs silhouette-adjacent
   samples. Interpolation is therefore not "free" either; nearest remains the
   correct default for this pipeline.
4. Mesh-normal error stays ~51° for every variant (even the GT-depth mesh
   scores 38.9° on this marching-cubes/BVH normal metric), so it cannot
   discriminate the ablations; F-score/accuracy/completeness can, and they are
   flat.

### E1 conclusion so far

The waviness lives in the predicted depth maps themselves as a view-consistent,
mid-frequency surface-shape error. It survives every regrid, filter, sampling
and robust-fusion variant. The next phase must therefore change the depth
*values*, using the multi-view RGB evidence (P1 depth refinement / E2 COLMAP
PatchMatch baseline), not the fusion machinery.

### Visual review (renders + close-ups)

Grey-matcap (clay) renders, identical cameras: 4 GT train views (indices
0/4/8/12, `data/scenes/nerf_lego_gt16/geometry/gt/cameras.json`) and 4 heldout
test views in source gauge (`outputs/lego_heldout_source/cameras.json`).
Renders: `deliverables/lego_p05/renders/<case>/{train,heldout}/view_*.png`;
3× close-up crops: `deliverables/lego_p05/crops/<case>_{studs_top,posts,wheels,base}.png`.

What is actually seen (reviewed image by image; reviewer: Kimi-K3, this session):

1. **Waviness morphology**: on predicted-depth meshes the surface is covered by
   isotropic, blobby undulation a few voxels across ("rock/coral" texture on the
   posts, smooth "dunes" on the base plate) plus a faint periodic diagonal
   banding on large planar regions. There are **no axis-aligned stripes or
   staircase blocks** anywhere, and the pattern is pixel-for-pixel identical
   between `repro_nearest`, `bilinear_both` and `bilateral`. A nearest-regrid
   staircase artifact would be axis-aligned to the image lattice and would
   visibly change with interpolation — it does not, consistent with the Part A
   numbers (Δ ≤ 0.1°).
2. **Thin structures**: the two vertical posts, the stud rows and the wheel
   holes are melted/merged in *every* predicted-depth variant
   (`crops/repro_nearest_posts.png` vs `crops/gt_reference_posts.png`); the
   combined case shows no recovery. This detail loss — not silhouette error —
   is the dominant visual defect and matches the precision gap (0.554 vs
   0.919 for GT depth).
3. **Filter/robust cases**: `bilateral` crops are indistinguishable from
   `repro_nearest`; `combined` changes a few dune positions on the base plate
   but keeps the same roughness and loses a little edge definition — matching
   its slightly lower F-score.
4. **Heldout views**: silhouettes are good for all cases (IoU ≈ 0.938 below);
   the defects are internal surface quality, invisible in IoU.
5. **Undulation amplitude**: repro accuracy mean 1.403 voxels / p90 3.56
   voxels vs 0.360 / 0.89 for the GT-depth mesh — i.e. typical surface
   deviation ≈ 0.016 source units (1.4 voxels), quadrupling the GT-depth
   baseline. This is the amplitude the next phase must remove.

## Conclusions and next steps

**How much of the 32.16° normal error is resampling?** ≈0.1° (0.3%). Nearest →
bilinear regrid changes 32.156° → 32.073°; eliminating resampling entirely
(native-grid normals) gives 32.050°. Stencil choice (radius 1 vs 2) and
edge-preserving bilateral sweeps each move ≤0.5°. The remaining ~32° is
intrinsic to the predicted depth: a locally smooth (pixel roughness 0.0096%,
smoother than GT), view-consistent, mid-frequency surface warp — sub-percent
amplitude at ~20–100 px wavelength — that tilts normals without moving depth
statistics. Visual review confirms blobby isotropic undulation with no
staircase/moiré signature, identical across all resampling variants.

**Best combination?** None of the tested fusion-side combinations beats the
baseline: every variant is within −0.01 F-score of 0.6206 and the full
combination is worst (0.6102). The baseline (nearest regrid + nearest
projective fusion + no filter) remains correct; bilinear is slightly harmful at
silhouettes (gt-pose case: 0.5968 vs 0.6021). The P0 two-voxel consistency
filter (F 0.6927, recall 0.623) remains the strongest *precision* option but
keeps its completeness cost; it is orthogonal to this phase and unchanged.

**Gap to the E2 target** (F@1vox > 0.70 with recall ≥ the 0.705 raw baseline,
lower normal error): current best-at-full-recall is 0.6206 / recall 0.705.
Needed: +0.08 F-score without losing recall. This phase shows that gap cannot
be closed by resampling, filtering, robust fusion or their combination — only
by improving the depth values themselves.

**Recommended order for the next phase:**

1. First fix **depth estimation**, not fusion: E2 external baseline
   `VGGT cameras → COLMAP PatchMatch → same TSDF/eval` gives an independent
   multi-view measurement of whether high-resolution RGB matching beats Omega's
   1.24%-error depth. In parallel, P1 (photometric + multi-view consistency
   refinement of the Omega depth, with the Omega depth as prior) is the main
   line; the P0.5 infrastructure (parameterized switches) already supports
   dropping refined depth into the identical protocol.
2. Do **not** invest further in TSDF/fusion tricks or small-window depth
   filters for this defect — measured here to be neutral at best.
3. Capture-side controlled experiments (rendering capture schemes from the GT
   scene) to quantify how view count / overlap / resolution / lighting move
   Omega's depth and normal error — tracked in
   `docs/experiments/CAPTURE_GUIDELINES.md` (shooting spec) and
   `docs/experiments/GEOMETRY_OPTIMIZATION.md` (geometry roadmap), both seeded
   from these results.
